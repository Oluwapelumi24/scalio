# Salon ops leave Veno half-built
Industry practice and leading salon platforms treat **ownership, bookability, and attendance as three separate axes**, then layer commissions or booth rent on top of a multi-staff calendar. Veno already ships the **right org ladder** (owner → manager → practitioner → front desk), a **singular owner account**, and a **clock policy that matches labor norms**—practitioners and front desk clock; owners and managers oversee. Where Veno diverges from what competitors and trade guidance treat as day-1 ops is pay math and employment-model flexibility: **no commissions, no tips, no booth-rent workflows**, and **coarse fixed roles** instead of customizable permission matrices. For a founder shipping into employee, commission, and chair-renter floors, that means the product is **aligned on who runs the floor and who clocks**, **partial on how privilege and schedules work**, and **gapped on how money moves to staff**. Nigeria peers (Buk.ng, startbuddi) share Veno’s thinner payroll surface; global leaders (Fresha, Vagaro, Square, Mindbody) do not.

## Competitors encode ownership separate from the chair
Across Fresha, Booksy, Vagaro, Square Appointments, Mindbody, Buk.ng, and startbuddi, the **first account is a singular Owner** with exclusive billing and security powers; stylists are invited team members with calendars and narrower access. Fresha allows only **one workspace owner**, transferable to another member who then drops to a high-access staff role ([Fresha ownership](https://www.fresha.com/help-center/knowledge-base/workspace-settings/100665-transfer-workspace-ownership-access-1)). Booksy auto-assigns Owner to the creator and keeps subscription and security alerts off Managers ([Booksy permissions](https://support.booksy.com/hc/en-us/articles/16535688940946-How-do-I-manage-staff-permissions)). Vagaro’s **business owner always has View and Modify on every permission** and has **no access level**—Admin is for trusted employees when settings need changing ([Vagaro access levels](https://support.vagaro.com/hc/en-us/articles/18977275541531-Configure-Access-Levels-and-Employee-Permissions)). Mindbody and startbuddi similarly reserve subscription, bank, and role-change powers to the owner login ([Mindbody staff permissions](https://support.mindbodyonline.com/s/article/203253743-Staff-permissions-explained?language=en_US); [startbuddi seats and roles](https://startbuddi.com/help/billing-and-payments/how-to-manage-seats-and-roles/)).

Role language converges on the same floor jobs trade guides describe. Entrepreneur’s staffing stack—owner/operator, salon manager, stylists, receptionist—is the operational template Nigeria job ads still use for Salon Manager and Front Desk roles ([NBC News / Entrepreneur](https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna55975680); [Jobberman front desk](https://www.jobberman.com/listings/front-desk-officer-rrj9p8)). Software maps that into **Owner/Admin, Manager, Front Desk/Reception, Service Provider**, with Vagaro and Square adding explicit **Self-Employed / Service Provider** vs commission employee splits ([Vagaro employee types](https://support.vagaro.com/hc/en-us/articles/17310859833499-What-are-Employee-Types-); [Square bookable team](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/5350-create-staff-member-profiles-for-square-appointments)). Boulevard’s model (widely cited as salon ops best practice) further **separates Staff Role** (does this person perform services and show on the calendar?) from **Permission Groups** (what can they do in the product)—so a manager who still takes clients can be bookable without Admin rights ([Boulevard staff roles](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/5941382-staff-roles); [Boulevard default roles](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/13382959-default-roles-permission-groups)).

Attendance follows the same separation. Labor and payroll vendors treat the **appointment book as capacity, not a timesheet**: booked hours show who was scheduled, not who showed or when they left ([Punch salon time tracking](https://www.punchapp.io/blog/salon-barbershop-time-tracking-payroll-guide); [EZTime salons](https://eztime.com/industries/salons-and-spas/)). US equity owners actively managing are generally **exempt from daily hours-worked payroll clocks** ([DOL FOH Ch. 22](https://www.dol.gov/sites/dolgov/files/WHD/legacy/files/FOH_Ch22.pdf)), while nonexempt stylists and front desk still need **clocked presence** for minimum wage, overtime, and greater-of hourly vs commission checks ([Mangomint hourly vs salary](https://www.mangomint.com/blog/hourly-vs-salary-payroll/); [Alaska DOL nail salon letter](https://labor.alaska.gov/lss/forms/nail_salon_letter.pdf)). True booth renters **set their own hours and do not clock**—requiring fixed attendance is an IRS/HMRC control factor toward employee status ([IRS Pub 4902](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p4902.pdf); [GOV.UK hair and beauty status](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/check-employment-status-if-you-work-in-hair-and-beauty)). Product framing matches: Mindbody positions Time Clock as ideal for staff **not paid to perform services** (front desk); Fresha, Vagaro, and Square enable timesheets **per hourly member** and have managers **edit and clock others** rather than mandating owner punches ([Mindbody Time Clock permissions](https://support.mindbodyonline.com/s/article/Staff-permissions-for-each-MINDBODY-software-level-Time-Clock?language=en_US); [Fresha wages and timesheets](https://www.fresha.com/help-center/knowledge-base/team/291-set-up-wages-for-team-members); [Vagaro time cards](https://support.vagaro.com/hc/en-us/articles/204347510-Set-Up-Time-Cards-and-Clock-In-Out)).

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fresha | Single transferable owner | Custom permission roles | Per-member timesheets | Pay runs + fixed/tiered commissions | Merchants for chair renters |

Day-1 table-stakes across those leaders are **multi-staff calendars, individual logins, a permission ladder, working hours/shifts, and commission tracking**. Full **clock → wages → payroll** is table-stakes mainly among POS-heavy global suites; **booth rent** is first-class on Vagaro (and chair-renter payment routing on Fresha Merchants), not universal ([Vagaro rent collection](https://support.vagaro.com/hc/en-us/articles/204347790-Set-Up-Automatic-Rent-Collection); [Fresha Merchants](https://www.fresha.com/help-center/knowledge-base/payments/100647-merchants-overview); [Booksy team management](https://biz.booksy.com/features/team-management)).

## Aligned: Veno already matches the floor’s org and clock rules
Veno’s registration path creates a `vendors` business and a `staff` row with **`role: 'owner'`** in one flow, then gates public listing on platform approval—matching the industry singular-owner pattern without inventing dual founders as co-owners in product. The role enum is exactly the trade stack competitors name in salon language: **`owner`, `manager`, `practitioner`, `front_desk`**, with product copy that maps owner → money, manager → day ops, practitioner → own book, front desk → bookings and messages without money. Only owners delete staff and change payout bank details; managers share day-to-day mutate rights on team and storefront. That least-privilege ladder is the same shape as Booksy’s fixed levels and Boulevard’s Admin / Location Manager / Front Desk / Service Provider defaults ([Booksy permissions](https://support.booksy.com/hc/en-us/articles/16535688940946-How-do-I-manage-staff-permissions); [Boulevard default roles](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/13382959-default-roles-permission-groups)).

Attendance is where Veno is unusually well aligned with labor norms rather than with “everyone clocks” product defaults. **`CLOCKABLE_ROLES` are only `practitioner` and `front_desk`**. Owners and managers cannot clock; attempting to clock them returns a forbidden response with explicit copy that they do not clock in. They get the **team live view**, can clock clockable staff on their behalf (`source: 'self' | 'manager'`), and see attendance rates only for those roles. That matches Punch/Mangomint guidance that clocks serve payroll labor, IRS/HMRC guidance that booth renters and owners are not wage-hour punch populations, and Mindbody/Fresha framing that clocks are for hourly/front-desk work while managers administer timesheets ([Punch](https://www.punchapp.io/blog/salon-barbershop-time-tracking-payroll-guide); [Mindbody Time Clock](https://support.mindbodyonline.com/s/article/Staff-permissions-for-each-MINDBODY-software-level-Time-Clock?language=en_US)). Separately, Veno already ships the **multi-staff calendar**, walk-ins, weekly schedules, time off, practitioner-scoped own bookings, and front-desk/manager full-book visibility that every competitor treats as non-negotiable day-1 ops.

## Partial: privilege and schedules exist, but without the dual axis leaders use
Veno’s permissions are **coarse Nest `@Roles` gates and sidebar redirects**—four fixed roles, no custom roles, no per-action ACL matrix. Fresha ships **unlimited custom permission roles** grouped by Calendar, Sales, Clients, Reports, Team, and more ([Fresha create permission roles](https://www.fresha.com/help-center/knowledge-base/team/100687-create-permission-roles)). Vagaro splits **Employee Type** (Service Provider vs Account Admin) from **Access Levels**, so bookability and privilege are independent ([Vagaro access levels](https://support.vagaro.com/hc/en-us/articles/18977275541531-Configure-Access-Levels-and-Employee-Permissions)). Square and Mindbody likewise allow custom or package-gated permission sets beyond a four-rung ladder ([Square permission sets](https://squareup.com/help/gb/en/article/5822-employee-permissions)). Veno’s four roles get the org chart right for a first release, but founders cannot express common mid-size patterns without code changes: a bookable manager with limited finance, a front-desk lead with discount caps, an accountant with ledger-only access, or a self-employed provider on the calendar without employee timesheets.

The same “almost” pattern shows up in how Veno treats the working owner as a calendar actor. Industry guidance expects **owner-operators who take clients to appear on the appointment book** while remaining off the wage clock ([Boulevard staff roles](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/5941382-staff-roles); [NBC News / Entrepreneur](https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna55975680)). Veno correctly keeps owners off the clock, and owners/managers can see and run the full calendar—but the product does **not** document a first-class “owner also performs services / show on calendar as provider” toggle separate from the owner role. Practitioners are the bookable service role; calendar-only staff can be created without login. That covers hired chairs better than owner-operator duality. Relative to Nigeria peers, Veno is **ahead on attendance** (Buk.ng and startbuddi document schedules and roles, not salon-style shift clocks) and **peer-level on deposits and single-location ops**, while still behind Buk.ng’s explicit Finance/Marketer role granularity ([Buk.ng staff permissions](https://buk.ng/help/set-staff-permissions); [startbuddi seats](https://startbuddi.com/help/billing-and-payments/how-to-manage-seats-and-roles/)).

Payroll and performance sit in the same partial band. Veno records **monthly fixed salary** (`monthlySalaryKobo`), staff bank details, and owner mark-paid/skip flows without moving money—useful audit for salaried floors, incomplete for the commission-majority beauty labor model Modern Salon and Entrepreneur still describe as base-and/or-commission or straight commission ranges ([Modern Salon compensation](https://www.modernsalon.com/news/compensating-your-staff); [NBC News / Entrepreneur](https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna55975680)). Performance already folds **clocked ÷ scheduled hours** for practitioners and front desk once the business has first clocked—good ops signal—but without commission or tip inputs it cannot power the “greater of hourly vs commission” or pay-run stacks Fresha and Square market as core ([Fresha pay runs](https://www.fresha.com/help-center/knowledge-base/team/102232-enable-pay-runs-and-set-your-pay-period); [Square commissions](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/7145-set-up-commission-tracking)).

## Gaps: commissions, booth rent, and employment-model flexibility are missing
The clearest competitive gaps are features the notes mark **absent from Veno code**, not merely thin. **Staff commissions / service percent splits** do not exist—payroll schema is monthly salary only, despite marketing mentions of commission elsewhere. Fresha, Booksy, Square, Vagaro, and Mindbody all treat commission tracking as day-1 or near-day-1 ([Fresha commissions](https://www.fresha.com/help-center/knowledge-base/team/98-set-up-commissions-for-team-members); [Booksy team management](https://biz.booksy.com/features/team-management)). **Tip tracking and tip pools** are likewise absent, while Booksy calculates tips at checkout and Square imports tips into payroll ([Square run payroll](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/5855-run-payroll)). **Booth / chair rental**—recurring rent, contracts, card-on-file collection, Self-Employed permission profiles—is first-class on Vagaro and supported via Fresha Merchants for routing client payments to individual wallets; Veno has **no rent schema or services** ([Vagaro rent collection](https://support.vagaro.com/hc/en-us/articles/204347790-Set-Up-Automatic-Rent-Collection); [Fresha Merchants](https://www.fresha.com/help-center/knowledge-base/payments/100647-merchants-overview)). Without a self-employed provider type, Veno cannot cleanly put a renter on a shared calendar **without** forcing them into the employee attendance path industry advisors say creates classification risk ([IRS Pub 4902](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p4902.pdf); [Chau Tran CPA booth vs employee](https://www.chautrancpa.com/blog/booth-rent-vs-employee-salon-classification.html)).

Other gaps matter for growth salons even if they are not universal day-1 in Africa docs: **multi-location / branches** is only a `coming_soon` personalisation module; **retail / inventory / POS product sales** likewise; gift cards and memberships do not appear as ops features. Automated salary disbursement is absent by design (owner pays off-platform and marks paid)—acceptable for Nigeria SMB cashflow culture shared with Buk.ng/startbuddi deposit models, but a gap versus Fresha pay runs and Square/Vagaro payroll if Veno courts US/global employee salons ([Fresha paying your team](https://www.fresha.com/help-center/knowledge-base/team/587-paying-your-team-overview); [Buk.ng for salons](https://buk.ng/for/salons)). Booksy is the important counter-example that **commissions without a time clock** can still be a viable marketplace-led posture; Veno already has the clock, so the sharper miss is **pay math**, not attendance hardware ([Booksy Biz](https://biz.booksy.com/)).

Uncertainty remains where the notes are thin: there is **no high-quality census** of how many small salons are owner-operator versus manager-run, nor a trade survey of “do owners clock in?”—those conclusions rest on labor rules plus vendor models. Nigeria-specific booth-rental labor guidance and UK punch-clock prevalence are also under-documented; treat Africa competitive parity as **as documented**, not exhaustive. Fresha’s 2024 booth-rent preview should be verified live before claiming feature parity with Vagaro’s automatic rent collection.

## Conclusion
Veno does not need to invent a new salon org model—the industry already agrees on singular ownership, a manager layer, bookable practitioners, front desk without money, and clocks for wage labor rather than for equity owners. The strategic choice is whether to stay **Nigeria-peer complete** (roles + calendar + deposits + optional attendance) or to close the **global table-stakes pay stack** (commissions first, then tips and a self-employed/booth path that keeps renters off employee timesheets). The highest-leverage next move for a founder is **commission tracking on the existing payroll surface**, because it unlocks the employment model most salons already run without undoing the attendance policy Veno correctly shipped; the dual-axis bookability-vs-permissions split is the structural follow-on that makes owner-operators and chair renters fit the same product without expanding the role enum into chaos.
